## Your first day

You will be given an email address and a **temporary password**, in person or by
phone. It will not arrive by email, on purpose: sending a password by email would
put it somewhere that is easier to steal than to protect.

1. Open the portal address and sign in with the temporary password.
2. You will be asked to set a password of your own straight away. Until you do, the
   portal deliberately lets you reach only your account and your onboarding. Client
   work stays closed. This is so that a password somebody else typed for you cannot
   be used to reach client records.
3. Choose something at least 12 characters long, mixing upper case, lower case and
   digits or symbols. A short phrase you can remember beats a short jumble you
   cannot.

Then you land on **My onboarding**, which is the only page you need on day one. It
shows, in order:

- A progress bar covering three things: your own steps, the documents you owe a
  response to, and whether your personal details are complete.
- **The welcome message** from the Managing Director.
- **Documents to read and sign.** Your contract comes first, then handbook
  policies.
- **Your personal details**, with a list of exactly what is still missing.
- **Your own steps**, which you tick as you go.
- **What the firm is doing for you**, which you can see but not tick, so you know
  the firm is doing its part too.

Nothing here blocks you from working. If your contract is unsigned you can still
reach your work queue. The outstanding items simply stay visible, to you and to
whoever is looking after your onboarding.

### Signing your contract, and the handbook

Open the document from your onboarding page. Three things are true of every document
you sign here:

- **The button stays disabled until you have scrolled to the end.** This is
  deliberate. You are agreeing to the whole thing.
- **You tick an attestation and type your full name.** The typed name has to match
  the name on your account. Capitals and spacing are forgiven; a different name is
  not.
- **What is recorded** is the date and time, your computer's address, and a
  fingerprint of the exact words you agreed to. If a policy is later amended, that
  fingerprint proves what you originally signed, and the amended version comes back
  to you to acknowledge again.

You can return to anything you have signed at any time. It is listed at the foot of
your onboarding page and in the handbook.

## Finding your way around

Everything is in the left sidebar, in three groups.

**Work** is client work: your dashboard, deliverables, clients, engagements, job
templates and reports.

**My portal** is you: your onboarding, the employee handbook, and your own details.

**Administration** appears only if your grade includes it: the people directory,
accounts and grades, and the handbook and welcome authoring.

Top right you have your **Inbox**, which carries a count of anything unread, the
appearance control, and your own account.

The word **deliverable** is used throughout for a single piece of client work: one
VAT return, one set of accounts, one filing. Each one has a reference like
`TSK-000042`.

## Doing a piece of client work

Work reaches you in one of two ways: it appears on your **Dashboard** under your own
queue, or somebody tells you about it and you find it under **Deliverables**.

Open it. A deliverable page has the client and the deadlines at the top, then the
procedures, then discussion, review points, time and documents.

### The two dates, which are not the same thing

- **Internal target** is when the firm wants it finished.
- **Statutory deadline** is when it must be filed.

The internal target is deliberately earlier, so that a review round does not eat
into the legal deadline. Work to the internal date.

### Getting through it

1. Press **Start work**. The status moves to In progress, so everyone can see it is
   under way rather than sitting untouched.
2. Work through the **procedures**, ticking each as you complete it. Some are marked
   **Mandatory**. Those are not suggestions: **you cannot submit until every
   mandatory procedure is ticked.** The portal will tell you which one is missing.
3. **Log your time** as you go, with a short narrative. It is much easier now than
   at the end of the month.
4. **Attach the working papers** by adding links to where they live. The portal
   holds links rather than the files themselves, so your documents stay in your
   document store.
5. Use **Discussion** for anything the next person needs to know. Everyone involved
   is notified, and by email too if the firm has that switched on.
6. Press **Submit for review** when it is done.

Once submitted, it is out of your hands until the reviewer looks at it. If you
realise you submitted too early, **Recall** brings it back to you.

### If it comes back for rework

You will get an inbox message, and an email if you have those on. The deliverable
returns to you with **review points**: numbered findings, each with a severity and
usually a reference to the working paper at issue.

Work through them:

1. Fix the underlying work.
2. **Answer each point** in the portal, saying what you did. This matters: the
   answer is the record of how the point was cleared.
3. Press **Resubmit for review**.

**Every must-fix point needs an answer before you can resubmit.** If the button is
refusing, that is why, and the portal names the point that is still unanswered.
Points marked as advisory or a comment do not block you, though it is polite to
answer them.

### If you are waiting on the client

Use **Awaiting client** rather than leaving it In progress. It keeps the reports
honest about where the delay actually is, which matters when someone asks why a
filing is late. **Resume** when the information arrives.

## Reviewing someone else's work

You can review at **Senior Associate** grade and above.

Work waiting for you appears on your **Dashboard**. Open it and press **Begin
review**, which moves it to Under review and tells the preparer you have picked it
up.

### Raising review points

For each finding, press **Raise a review point** and give:

- **What is wrong**, plainly. The preparer has to be able to act on it without
  asking you what you meant.
- **A severity.** Use **Must fix** for anything that must change before sign-off,
  and the lighter severities for improvements and observations. This is not
  decoration: must-fix points block resubmission and block approval, and the others
  do not.
- **A reference** to the working paper or figure at issue, if there is one.

Then either **Return for rework**, which sends it back to the preparer, or approve
it if there was nothing material.

**Returning for rework needs at least one review point and a note.** Sending work
back with no explanation wastes the preparer's time, so the portal does not allow
it.

### Closing the loop

When the work comes back, the preparer's answers sit under each point. For each one,
either **Resolve** it, if you are satisfied, or **Reopen** it if you are not.

Then **Approve**. **You cannot approve while a must-fix point is unresolved.** Waive
it explicitly if you have decided it does not matter after all. Waiving is recorded
with your name against it, which is the point.

### Two things you cannot do

**You cannot review your own work.** No grade overrides this, including
Administrator. If you prepared it, someone else signs it off.

**An Associate cannot be named as reviewer.** If you are trying to assign a reviewer
and someone is missing from the list, that is why.

## Setting work up (Managers and above)

### Creating a deliverable

**Deliverables → New deliverable**, at Senior Associate grade and above. You need
the client, a title, a service line and the dates. Everything else can follow.

Better, for anything recurring: create it from a **job template**, which brings the
standard procedures and the statutory deadline rule with it.

### Assigning it

Assigning work needs **Manager** grade. On the deliverable, set:

- **Assignee**: who is doing it.
- **Reviewer**: who signs it off. Senior Associate grade or above, and never the
  same person as the assignee.

Leave the reviewer blank if you have not decided. Any Senior Associate can pick it
up when it is submitted, which is often more practical than naming someone in
advance.

### Closing and reopening

**Manager** grade closes an approved file. Closing a recurring job **automatically
creates the next period's job**, with the dates rolled forward, so a monthly filing
never has to be set up twice.

A closed file is read only. Only a **Partner** can reopen one, and the reopening is
recorded.

## Job templates and the filing calendar

**Job templates** is where the firm's standard jobs live: VAT returns, PAYE,
withholding tax, corporate tax and the rest. Fourteen come ready made.

**Anyone at Manager grade can add and change them.**

### Editing a template

Press **Edit** on any template. You can set the name, service line, how often it
recurs, the default priority, a short label, budget hours and a description, plus
the two things that matter most:

**Procedures.** The tick-list that appears on every job made from this template.
Add, remove and reorder them, and mark the ones that **must** be ticked before
submission. Think of this as the firm's method for that job, written down once.

**The filing deadline.** Entered the way the tax rules are written: so many months
after the period ends, on such a day. A VAT return due on the 21st of the following
month is "1 month, day 21". A worked example under the fields shows the date that
produces, so you can check it. If a day falls beyond the end of a short month it is
pulled back to the last day.

Switch the deadline off entirely for internal work with no filing date, such as
management accounts.

> **These deadlines are defaults, not advice.** They were set as sensible starting
> points. Check each against current Ghana Revenue Authority rules and adjust. That
> is a job for someone who knows the rules, not a technical job.

### Retiring a template

There is no delete, deliberately. **Retire** it instead: it stops appearing when new
work is created and cannot generate jobs, but every job already made from it carries
on untouched, so your history and audit trail stay intact. Retired templates are
behind the **Show retired** switch, and can be brought back.

### Generating a year of filings

**Generate jobs** on a template. Choose the clients, the period, and how many
periods to create. The portal works out each deadline from the rule, creates a
deliverable per client per period, and skips any that already exist so you cannot
double up by running it twice.

This is the fastest thing in the portal. One pass sets up a whole year of VAT
returns across every client.

## Clients and engagements

**Clients** holds the entity: its type, tax and registration numbers, financial year
end, risk rating, and which partner and manager are responsible. Creating and
editing needs **Manager** grade.

**Engagements** group deliverables under one piece of business: a signed engagement
letter, a fee and a budget. Use them when you want to see the whole of a client
relationship, or compare fee to time spent.

An engagement is optional. A deliverable can belong directly to a client.

## Your own records

**My details** is yours to maintain. You can change your contact details, emergency
contact, next of kin and qualifications.

You cannot change your job title, grade, start date or pay. Those are the firm's
record, and the portal refuses the attempt rather than quietly ignoring it.

Worth knowing about who sees what:

- Your **home address and date of birth** are visible to you and to HR
  administrators only. Deliberately **not** to your line manager, who has no working
  need for them.
- Your **pay and bank details** are visible to you and to Partners only, and are
  kept apart from the rest of your record so that an ordinary query cannot reach
  them.

**Employee handbook** holds every published policy. Anything you have not yet
acknowledged is marked. If a policy is amended it reappears here for you to
acknowledge again, and your earlier acknowledgement survives as a record of what you
originally agreed to.

## Running the firm's people side (Partners)

### Adding someone

**Accounts and grades → Add team member.** Set their grade carefully: it decides
everything they can do. See [Who can do what](#who-can-do-what).

A temporary password is shown **once**. Copy it and pass it on by phone or in
person, never in the same email as the link. They will be required to replace it
before they can reach anything.

### The rest of their record

**People → their name** is their personnel file: employment details, personal
details, qualifications, documents, and pay if you are a Partner.

**Start onboarding** creates their sixteen standard steps, split between them and
HR, and their onboarding page comes to life.

### The handbook and the welcome

**Handbook and welcome** is where you author what staff read.

Ten policies ship as **drafts**: conduct and ethics, client confidentiality,
independence and conflicts, anti-money laundering, IT security, leave, working
hours, dignity at work, performance, and grievance and disciplinary procedure.

> **They are drafting starting points, not finished legal instruments.** They ship
> as drafts so nothing reaches staff by accident. Have each checked against Ghanaian
> employment law and your professional body's requirements before publishing.

Nothing is visible to staff, and nothing can be signed, until you **Publish** it.

**Amending a published policy is the part worth understanding.** Editing the text of
a published policy raises its version, which puts it back in front of everyone to
acknowledge again. The previous acknowledgements are kept as the record of what was
agreed before. Editing a draft changes nothing, because nobody has agreed to it yet.

There is also a **Contract of Employment (template)**. Do not publish that one. Copy
it per person, fill in their details, and issue it to them individually.

**Logo and colours** is on the same screen. Pick a primary and secondary colour and
the whole portal recolours, previewing live before you save.

**The logo has two slots, and it is worth using both.** The portal has light pages
and dark ones: the sidebar and the sign-in panel are navy whatever theme you are in,
and in dark mode everything is dark. One logo cannot read well on both.

- **Logo** is your normal artwork, the dark-ink version, used on light pages.
- **Logo for dark backgrounds** is the same logo in white or light ink, used on the
  sidebar, the sign-in panel, and everywhere in dark mode.

Save both as **PNG files with a transparent background**. If the file carries its own
white rectangle, that rectangle shows as a white block anywhere the page behind it is
not white. Crop the empty space from around the artwork too, since the portal cannot
tell padding from logo and will shrink the whole thing to fit.

If you fill only the first slot nothing breaks: on dark surfaces the portal puts your
dark logo on a small white panel so it stays readable. It works, but the panel is
visible, and the second slot is how you get rid of it. The two previews on the screen
show you exactly which of the two is happening.

A wide logo is fine, and usually better, because the height is fixed and the width
follows your artwork. If your logo already includes the firm's name, the portal stops
printing the name beside it so it is not said twice.

## Reports

**Reports**, at Manager grade and above:

- **Overdue exposure**: what is late, by how much, and for whom.
- **Workload by person**: who is carrying how much.
- **Service line summary**: where the work actually is.
- **Review quality by preparer**: how many review points each person's work attracts.

A note on that last one. It is useful for spotting where coaching would help, and
misleading if read as a ranking. Somebody doing the hardest jobs will attract more
points than somebody doing the simplest.

## Who can do what

You do not grant permissions person by person. **You set a grade, and the grade
decides.** This is deliberate: individual permissions drift, get copied from the
wrong person, and quietly break the separation between preparing and reviewing.
Grades cannot.

| Grade | Can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Associate** | Prepare work, tick procedures, log time, answer review points. Cannot create deliverables and cannot review. |
| **Senior Associate** | The above, plus **create deliverables** and **review**: raise points, return for rework, approve. Can be named reviewer. |
| **Manager** | Plus **assign work**, create clients and engagements, add and edit job templates, generate filings, **close** files, see reports and the people directory. |
| **Partner** | Plus create and suspend accounts, reset passwords, publish handbook policies, edit the welcome and branding, see pay, and **reopen** a closed file. |
| **Administrator** | The same as Partner, for whoever runs the system. |

Each grade includes everything below it. Two rules override all of it and cannot be
turned off at any grade:

- **Nobody reviews their own work.**
- **An Associate can never be named reviewer.**

To change what somebody can do, change their grade in **Accounts and grades**.

## Settings, notifications and appearance

**Your inbox** carries a message whenever something needs you: work assigned,
submitted, returned, approved, or commented on. The count sits in the top bar.

**Email** can be sent as well, if the firm has set it up. You are emailed about a
deliverable when you are involved in it, meaning you are doing it, reviewing it,
created it, or have commented on it at any point. Commenting once is what keeps you
in the loop afterwards, which is why there is no separate list to subscribe to.

You are never emailed about your own actions. To turn email off entirely, go to
**My account** and untick it. Your inbox is unaffected and still shows everything.

**Appearance** is the three-button control in the top bar: Light, Dark, or Device,
which follows your phone or computer including its automatic evening switch. It is
your own choice, remembered in your browser, and does not affect anyone else.

**Your password** is changed under **My account**. Changing it signs you out of every
other browser you were signed in on, which is what you want if you are changing it
because you are worried.

## When something is not working

**A button I need is missing or greyed out.** Almost always the portal is telling you
that the action is not available yet, rather than that something is broken. Hover
over it: the reason is written there, and it is the same sentence the server would
give. Common cases: a mandatory procedure not ticked, a must-fix point unanswered,
or your grade not covering the action.

**"You are signed in with a temporary password."** Set your own password under My
account. Until then only your account and onboarding are reachable.

**I cannot see a client or a colleague's details.** The portal shows what your grade
and your involvement require and nothing more. If you need access you do not have,
ask a Partner rather than looking for a way round it.

**I forgot my password.** There is no self-service reset. Ask an administrator, who
can issue a new temporary password from Accounts and grades. They will pass it to
you by phone or in person.

**A deliverable is stuck and nobody can move it.** Check its status against the
review cycle: work in Submitted needs a reviewer to Begin review; work in Under
review needs the reviewer to return or approve it. If the named reviewer is away,
any Senior Associate can pick it up.

**Something looks genuinely wrong.** Note the deliverable reference, what you were
doing and what it said, and tell whoever administers the portal. The reference is
what makes it findable.
